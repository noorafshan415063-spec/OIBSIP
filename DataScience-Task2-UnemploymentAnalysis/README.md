## 1. Project Overview

This project focuses on analysing unemployment trends in India using Python. The objective is to explore unemployment rates across different Indian regions, understand changes over time, and compare rural and urban unemployment.

The project also examines unemployment rates before and during the COVID-19 period covered by the dataset.

## 2. Objectives

- Analyse monthly unemployment trends in India.
- Compare unemployment rates across different states.
- Identify the top 10 regions with the highest average unemployment rates.
- Study the relationship between unemployment, employment and labour participation.
- Compare rural and urban unemployment rates.
- Analyse unemployment before and during the COVID-19 period.

## 3. Dataset

**Dataset Name:** Unemployment in India

**Source:** [Kaggle – Unemployment in India](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/gokulrajkmv/unemployment-in-india)

- Records after cleaning: 740
- Regions covered: 28
- Time period: May 2019 – June 2020

## 4. Technologies and Libraries

- Python
- Jupyter Notebook
- Pandas
- NumPy
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn

## 5. Data Cleaning and Preprocessing

- Removed 28 completely empty rows.
- Removed unnecessary spaces from column names and categorical values.
- Converted the Date column into datetime format.
- Checked missing values and duplicate records.
- Prepared the dataset for exploratory data analysis.

## 6. Data Visualizations

The following visualizations were created:

1. Monthly Unemployment Trend in India
2. State-wise Unemployment Trend
3. Top 10 Regions by Average Unemployment Rate
4. Unemployment Correlation Heatmap
5. Pre-COVID vs During-COVID Comparison
6. Rural vs Urban Unemployment Comparison

## 7. Key Findings

- The overall average unemployment rate was approximately 11.79%.
- Tripura recorded the highest average unemployment rate at approximately 28.35%.
- The average unemployment rate was 9.51% before COVID-19 and 17.77% during the COVID-19 period.
- Urban areas recorded an average unemployment rate of 13.17%, compared with 10.32% in rural areas.

*Note: These findings describe the dataset period and should not be interpreted as current unemployment statistics. The COVID-period comparison is descriptive and does not establish causation.*

## 8. Conclusion

This project provided an opportunity to explore unemployment trends in India using Python.

Through data cleaning, exploratory data analysis and visualization, different unemployment patterns were examined across regions, time periods and areas.

The project helped strengthen practical skills in Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn and data interpretation.
